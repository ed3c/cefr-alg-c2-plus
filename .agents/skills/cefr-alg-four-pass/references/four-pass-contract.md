# Four-pass acquisition contract

## Adapted learning model

The supplied learning model describes a progression from passive recognition to active
generation. This project uses that model as a learning design hypothesis, not as a universal
neuroscience law and not as a claim that four exposures guarantee acquisition.

For technical articles, the useful invariant is narrower:

> The final pass must combine active generation with an explicit error comparison against a
> source-bound oracle.

Repeating the same readable text four times does not satisfy the contract.

## Technical-article adaptation

| Pass | Learner load | Visible support | Required operation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Context | low | full context | follow the situation and causal problem | receptive |
| 2 · Precision | medium | precise English transcript | bind terms to actors, conditions and decisions | receptive |
| 3 · Listening reconstruction | higher | transcript hidden by default | reconstruct the architecture from sound | retrieval |
| 4 · Active reconstruction | high but bounded | oracle hidden until attempt | retell/back-translate + mutate + compare | productive practice |

The source article remains the meaning oracle. The learner does not have to reproduce its
wording.

## Difficulty boundary

Do not increase difficulty by deleting the information needed to understand the problem.
If the precision pass cannot be understood with the supplied context, repair Pass 1 or split
the source into a smaller lesson. Difficulty should come from retrieval and generation, not
from missing premises.

## Review oracle

For each selected source claim, freeze a tuple:

`actor -> condition -> action/decision -> evidence/uncertainty -> consequence`

Pass 1 may simplify terminology but not this tuple. Pass 2 makes it explicit. Pass 3 keeps
the tuple in spoken form. Pass 4 turns the tuple into comparison criteria.

This adapts the preservation logic used by Soodles review-writing: clear actor/action,
condition before affected action, stable terms, supported reasoning, zero-context causality,
explicit acceptance boundaries, and preservation of uncertainty.

## What this does not prove

A completed pass does not prove CEFR C2 proficiency, long-term retention, pronunciation
quality, or conversion of every passive word into active vocabulary. The website records
practice interaction only when the learner chooses to create or download it.
