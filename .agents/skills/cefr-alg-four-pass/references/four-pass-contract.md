# Four-pass acquisition contract

## Adapted learning model

The supplied learning material describes a progression from passive recognition to active
generation. This project uses it as a learning-design hypothesis, not a universal neuroscience law
and not a claim that exactly four exposures guarantee acquisition.

The technical-article implementation preserves two invariants:

1. Passes 1-3 are receptive accumulation: encounter, recognition, and context-supported familiarity.
2. Pass 4 must combine active generation with explicit error comparison against a source-bound oracle.

Repeating the same text, transcript, audio, or video four times does not satisfy the contract.

## Technical-article adaptation

| Pass | Learner load | Default support | Required operation | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Encounter | low | clarity + full context | follow actors, stakes, causal problem | receptive |
| 2 · Recognition | medium | C2+ English + transcript + diagram | recognize terms and follow exact conditions | receptive |
| 3 · Contextual familiarity | medium/high | natural narration + English transcript + context | follow the argument directly in English; replay as needed | receptive |
| 4 · Active reconstruction | high but bounded | cue first; oracle hidden until attempt | shadow + retell/back-translate + mutate + compare | productive practice |

Pass 3 may offer transcript-off listening as an option. It must not require hidden-transcript
reconstruction or another output task as its completion condition.

## Passive and active vocabulary

Do not treat vocabulary as one undifferentiated list.

- **Passive encounter vocabulary** is broader. The learner needs rapid recognition in reading and
  listening. Favor repeated short encounters in real article contexts.
- **Active vocabulary** is smaller. Select phrases worth producing in engineering discussion,
  interviews, or writing. An active item must have a receptive precursor.

For a new passive term, prefer form + pronunciation when available + one contextually correct core
meaning. Later contexts may add senses. The source material's ten-second encounter procedure can be
used as a practice recipe, but ten seconds is not a universal contract value.

## Pass 4 production sequence

The productive pass can combine the source material's two high-resistance techniques:

1. listen to a source-bound segment;
2. delayed shadowing;
3. simultaneous shadowing when feasible;
4. retell or back-translate from meaning;
5. mutate a sentence/system premise and explain the consequence;
6. reveal and compare with the source-bound oracle.

Shadowing is not sufficient by itself. Mechanical imitation without semantic generation and
comparison remains incomplete.

## Difficulty boundary

Do not increase difficulty by removing premises needed for comprehension. If Pass 2/3 is not
understandable with the supplied context, repair Pass 1, narrow the lesson, or restore the missing
premise. Pass 4 resistance should come from retrieval, production, and comparison rather than
unrecoverable input.

## Review oracle

For each selected source claim, freeze:

`actor -> condition -> action/decision -> evidence/uncertainty -> consequence`

Pass 1 may simplify terminology but not the tuple. Pass 2 makes it precise. Pass 3 keeps it available
in natural spoken English. Pass 4 turns it into production and comparison criteria.

This adapts Soodles review-writing preservation logic: clear actor/action, condition before affected
action, stable terms, supported reasoning, zero-context causality, explicit acceptance boundaries,
and preservation of uncertainty.

## What this does not prove

A completed pass does not prove CEFR C2 proficiency, long-term retention, pronunciation quality, a
neurological threshold at the fourth exposure, or conversion of every passive word into active
vocabulary. The system structures practice; it does not certify acquisition.
